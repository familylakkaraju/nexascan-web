Privacy Policy

Your documents are yours. This page explains, in plain language, what NexaScan does with your data and what it doesn't.

Effective {{config:policyDate}}. Applies to the NexaScan app for iPhone and for Android.

The short version

  • Scanning, clean-up, text recognition, conversion and every PDF tool run on your phone, on iPhone and on Android.
  • NexaScan never uploads your documents, images or the text in them, to us or to anyone else.
  • There is no account, no sign-in and no advertising.
  • On iPhone, there is no usage analytics and no crash reporting at all. On Android, usage data and crash reports are off unless you turn them on. If you do, technical data goes to Google's Firebase. It never includes your documents, images, text or file names (section 5).
  • Your documents are kept in the app's private storage and are left out of iCloud backup on iPhone and Google cloud backup on Android. They leave the app only when you share them, save a copy elsewhere or open them in another app.
  • If you buy NexaScan Pro or redeem a Pro code, the app checks your Pro status with our licence service (and, for a purchase, with the App Store or Google Play). The licence service keeps the purchase's identifier and the code only as one-way hashes, with a random identifier for your installation. Nothing it keeps identifies you: no name, email address, Apple Account, Google account or documents (section 6).
  • When it opens, the app downloads the current NexaScan Pro plans and Free limits from the licence service. That request carries no identifier and nothing about you (section 6).
  • On Android, Google's text-recognition component (ML Kit) may send limited diagnostic data to Google. It never includes your images or text. On iPhone, text recognition uses Apple's Vision framework, which works on the phone and sends nothing (section 5).

2. What the app works with on your phone

  • Camera images, when you scan a document.
  • Files you choose: PDFs and images (JPG, JPEG, PNG) you pick in your phone's photo or file picker, or send to NexaScan from another app. A PDF you open in NexaScan from another app is kept as an unchanged copy in its Documents until you delete it.
  • The documents it creates: PDFs, images, Word and Excel files, and the text it recognises in them. Word and Excel files record the name of the file they were made from as their title.
  • Passwords you type in Protect, or to open a password-protected PDF in the Document Viewer. A Protect password is used once to encrypt the new copy; an opening password is used only to unlock that PDF. Neither is ever saved or logged.
  • An unlocked copy of a password-protected PDF, while you have it open in the Document Viewer. It is kept in the app's private storage and deleted when you close the document. If the app stops unexpectedly while it is open, the leftover copy is deleted when the app next clears its temporary files. Your original file stays protected.
  • A few app settings, such as whether you've seen the introduction and the app tour.
  • If you buy Pro or redeem a code: the Pro status the app has confirmed (where your Pro comes from, when it ends and when to check it again), and the private half of the installation key, which never leaves your phone (section 6). A code you type is sent to the licence service to redeem it and isn't saved on your phone.

All of this stays on your phone, and we never receive it. If your phone's iCloud or Google backup is turned on, the app settings may be included in that backup, but never your documents (section 9).

3. What we collect

We don't collect your documents, images, recognised text, file names or contacts, and NexaScan has no advertising SDK on either platform. On iPhone, the only data that can reach us is the licence data described in section 6, and only if you buy NexaScan Pro or redeem a code. On Android, there is the same licence data, and also usage data and crash reports, but only if you turn them on (section 5).

Usage data and crash reports (Android only)

The iPhone app contains no analytics or crash-reporting component, so it sends no usage data and no crash reports. This part applies only to the Android app.

On Android, both are off unless you turn them on, in every country. The app asks once, in the welcome tour when you first open it (or on the Home screen, if you leave the tour before answering): Allow turns both on, and you can also choose crash reports only, or Not now. You can change your answer at any time in Settings › Usage and crash data. We rely on your consent. Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics don't start until you agree, and neither sends anything before that. ML Kit, in the table above, is separate from this choice: it may send its diagnostic data whichever you choose. We use usage data to learn which features are used and where they fail, so we know what to improve, and crash reports to find and fix faults.

  • Usage data (Google Analytics for Firebase): which feature ran, such as a filter or a PDF tool; whether it finished, failed or was cancelled; how long it took; a range for the number of pages; a category for the type of error; which Pro screens were seen and the result of a purchase, restore or code (the outcome and the plan's billing period only); and whether you use Free or Pro. Google Analytics also records its own usage events, such as the first time the app is opened and how long it stays open.
  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics): the type of error, the names of the app's code it passed through, and the names of the features used just before. If the app stops responding, the report includes a list of what each part of the app was doing at that moment.
  • What Firebase adds: an installation ID it creates for this copy of the app (not your name or your phone's serial number), the app version and where it was installed from, the phone's model, the Android version, and your language, time zone and country settings. Crash reports also include the phone's manufacturer and hardware name, your mobile network code, the type of network connection, memory and storage in use, battery level, screen orientation and whether it is rooted. Google sees your IP address when data is sent, and Google Analytics uses a shortened form of it to estimate an approximate location, such as your country or region.
  • What is never sent: your documents, images, thumbnails, handwriting, recognised text, file names, folder paths, document titles, purchase tokens, receipts or redeem codes. The text of an error can contain a file's location, so the app removes it before a report is made.
  • Not collected at all: the advertising ID. Ad personalisation is off, and nothing is linked to Google Ads.

Turning a choice off stops sending from then on and deletes what is waiting on your phone, apart from anything being uploaded at that moment. The installation ID goes the next time the app starts, and agreeing again creates a new one. Data Google has already received is kept for up to 14 months (usage data) or 90 days (crash reports) and then deleted. Because this data isn't linked to your name, email address or Google account, we can't find yours from them, so we usually can't delete it sooner. Turning both choices off is the quickest way to stop it, and to break the link with anything sent before. You can still email {{email:privacyEmail}} with a question or request about this data, and we will answer it. Google processes it for us as a service provider, and may do so outside the UK. See Firebase's privacy information.

Local diagnostics (Android)

Whatever you choose above, the Android app keeps a small technical log in its private storage: feature names, timings and error types, up to about 1 MB, for up to 14 days. It holds no document content, file names or text, and it isn't included in Google backups. It leaves your phone only if you choose Share diagnostics or Save diagnostics in Settings, and you can clear it there at any time.

6. NexaScan Pro and the licence check

Buying Pro

NexaScan Pro is bought through the App Store on iPhone and through Google Play on Android. Apple or Google processes the payment, and we never receive your card or payment details. The two stores are separate: a subscription bought in one unlocks Pro only on that platform.

After a purchase, the app confirms the subscription with our licence service so that Pro can be unlocked, kept active and restored. The licence service checks it with the store, which always has the final say. On Android, while you use the app, it confirms the subscription again about once a week, so that renewals, cancellations and refunds reach your phone. On iPhone, it confirms the subscription when you buy or restore Pro, and when a renewal is due. For this, the app sends:

  • the purchase's identifier: on iPhone, the App Store's original transaction ID for your subscription; on Android, the purchase token that Google Play issues. The licence service uses it to check the subscription with the store, and stores it only as a one-way hash, which can't be turned back into the identifier;
  • an installation identifier, made from a random key that the app creates in your phone's secure key store (the iPhone's Secure Enclave, or Android's key store), together with a signature proving the request comes from this installation. It isn't based on your phone's serial number or other hardware IDs, your phone number, your Apple Account or your Google account.

On iPhone, the app also attaches a per-installation token to the purchase when you buy (Apple calls it the app account token). It is calculated one way from the installation identifier, so it names nothing that Apple could look up. It lets the licence service see which installation made the purchase, so that one purchase can't be shared across an unlimited number of devices. Your subscription still works on your other Apple devices signed in to the same Apple Account, up to a limit.

Redeeming a code

We give free Pro codes to the App Store's and Google Play's app reviewers, to testers, and to people we choose to give Pro as a gift or for promoting NexaScan. Codes are never sold, and they work on iPhone and Android. When you redeem one in Settings › NexaScan Pro, the app sends the code with the same installation identifier and signature described above.

The licence service stores codes only as one-way hashes, and records a grant for your installation (see "What the licence service keeps" below). Neither a code nor the name we give it contains a name, email address, phone number or anything else that identifies a person. The names describe what a code is for, such as "Google Play review".

While you have Pro from a code, the app checks your Pro status with the licence service more frequently than for a purchase, so that a new end date or a withdrawn code reaches your phone. When a check is due, it can happen when you open or return to the app, open the NexaScan Pro screen or a Pro tool, or while the app stays open.

If we send you a code by email or message, we use your address only to send it. The licence service doesn't record who received a code.

Plans and Free limits

When you open the app, and when you return to it after a while, it asks the licence service for the current NexaScan Pro plans and Free limits, so that they can be updated without a new version of the app. Every copy of the app does this, Free or Pro. The request names only the app: it carries no installation identifier, purchase, code or anything about you.

What the licence service keeps

The licence service keeps only these records:

  • Your installation: the installation identifier with its public key, and a link to the purchase or code that gives it Pro.
  • A purchase: the purchase's identifier (the App Store's transaction ID or Google Play's purchase token) as a one-way hash, which store it came from, and the state of the subscription: the plan, when it started, when the paid period ends, and whether it renews.
  • Our list of codes: each code as a one-way hash, with details of the code itself: the name we gave it, whether it's for test or release versions of NexaScan, what it's for (such as app review or testing), when it ends, whether it's switched on for new redemptions, how many installations may redeem it and how many have, and when it was created and last changed.
  • A grant, for each installation that redeems a code: which code it came from (the code's hash and name), when it was redeemed, when it ends, and whether we withdrew it. The code's list of redemptions also records the date and a shortened fingerprint of the installation identifier.

It doesn't receive or store your name, email address, Apple Account, Google account, payment details, IP address, documents or their text, and you don't need an account. It runs on Amazon Web Services in the United Kingdom (London region), for both platforms.

We keep installation and purchase records while your subscription is active, and afterwards so that Pro can be restored on this phone or a new one. If a code has been redeemed, its code record and its grant records are kept after the code expires, is switched off or is withdrawn, as the record of how the code was used. None of these records contains your name, email address or account, so we can't find them from your name or email address. On Android, uninstalling NexaScan deletes the key on your phone, so that installation identifier can't be used again. On iPhone, the key stays on that iPhone only, never in iCloud or a backup, and iOS may keep it when the app is deleted, so a reinstalled app can use it again. The licence service's own logs are deleted automatically within 30 days.

If you never buy Pro or redeem a code, the app sends the licence service nothing about your installation: its only contact is the plans and Free limits request above.

9. Where your documents are stored

  • Documents you save are kept in NexaScan's private app storage. Other apps can't read this area, and it is protected by your phone's own security and encryption.
  • On iPhone, this storage, and your saved signature and initials, are excluded from iCloud backup. The installation key is never backed up. App settings, such as whether you've seen the tour, can be included in iCloud backup.
  • On Android, this storage is excluded from Google cloud backup. On Android 12 and later, a direct device-to-device transfer to a new phone may copy it. The installation identifier and licence information are excluded from both. App settings, such as whether you've seen the tour, can be included in Google cloud backup.
  • Working files are kept in the app's private cache while a task runs and are deleted afterwards. Leftover temporary files more than a day old are removed the next time you open NexaScan.
  • NexaScan doesn't save documents to your photo library, gallery or shared folders by itself. They leave the app only when you share them, save a copy elsewhere or open them in another app.

10. Retention and deletion

Your documents stay on your phone until you delete them. You can delete any document in the app, and deleting or uninstalling NexaScan removes all its documents and settings (on Android, so does clearing its storage in Settings). We hold no copy of your documents. The licence service holds no name, email address or account, so we can't look up a person's licence record (section 6). For any question about it, email {{email:privacyEmail}}. On Android, usage data and crash reports are covered in section 5: turning them off deletes what is waiting on your phone, and Google deletes what it has received after 14 months (usage data) or 90 days (crash reports).

11. Security

  • Processing happens on the phone, so your documents don't travel over the Internet when you use NexaScan.
  • The licence check, code redemption and the plans and Free limits request (section 6), and on Android usage data and crash reports (section 5), travel over an encrypted HTTPS connection.
  • Protect (Pro) encrypts a new copy of the PDF with AES-256. Your original file is unchanged, and your password isn't stored. On Android, the Protect screen blocks screenshots while you type.
  • The password you type to open a protected PDF in the Document Viewer isn't stored. On Android, its password box also blocks screenshots.
  • NexaScan doesn't add its own encryption to documents in its storage. They rely on your phone's app sandbox and device encryption. For sensitive files, use Protect.

12. Children

NexaScan is a general-purpose document tool, suitable for all ages. It isn't directed at children, and it doesn't knowingly collect data from anyone, including children.
